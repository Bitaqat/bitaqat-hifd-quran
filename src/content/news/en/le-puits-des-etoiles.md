Where do the recitation cards come from? The new novel in the series answers in its own way —
the way of a tale. **The Well of Stars — Origins of the Seal of Stars** is out, in paperback and
Kindle eBook. The French edition is live today; the English edition is going live within days.

Granada, late in the year 1491. In a ruined garden, a young master with too hurried a heart leans
over a waterless well — and the bottom of the well opens onto a sky of stars. It is the story of
Idris, fallen a thousand years into the future among the last reciters of the worlds; of the
black ships that hunt the bearers of the Book; and of a treasure map barred with six wax seals,
sealed beneath a hill to wait for its hour.

## What makes this novel special

Like its elder, it is written for readers aged 10 and up — and for everyone else. Fifteen plates
illustrate it, and the seven verses of Al-Fatiha serve as its backbone: each chapter walks
alongside one of them, in order. Every chapter closes with a sourced panel — verses and hadiths
with full references, in approximate renderings of the meaning — and the part that is true is
always explicitly separated from the part that is tale.

The two novels can be read in either order: *The Well of Stars* begins before *The Seal of
Stars* and ends at its first page, giving away none of the surprises of either.
